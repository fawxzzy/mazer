- WHY it changed: WebKit had to be measured before any fix. WebKit (Playwright 2311 on Windows, a stand-in for Safari) at level 300, 390x844 at 3x, ranked costs of a walking frame: (1) about 86 to 105 ms is NOT the trail: with trail painting switched off the frame still takes that long at 3x and about 50 ms at 2x; hiding the board's tile layer (one 2.75 Mpx texture) alone saves about 30 ms, the 1170x2532 main canvas gets about 258 image draws a frame; (2) the bloom (blurred glow): about 54 to 61 ms; (3) the sparkle keylines: about 29 to 35 ms; (4) the thread: about 15 to 22 ms; (5) the sparkle fills: about 12 to 16 ms; (6) sparkle animation: about 7 ms. Idle, the redraw gate already spaces trail repaints to about 240 ms on WebKit. So on Safari the trail is about half of a long-trail frame and the 3x canvas size is the other half. Every option keeps the stars real, pointed and rainbow-shifting (no glow stand-in).
- Rule: Measure WebKit with frame intervals plus layer ablation (switch one layer off, same page, same trail), never with Chromium profiles: WebKit defers raster, so script timings miss the cost, and a Chromium-only win (#700) can be worth nothing on an iPhone. Never edit `src/` while a dev-server bench runs (Vite reloads the page and the run dies). Fewer, longer round-capped strokes are dimmer than many short overlapping ones: butt-cap the pieces, end them only on straight runs, and match brightness by measured coverage, not by eye.

A's coarse glow matched Today at gain 2.5 (alpha coverage 0.98 of Today). Screenshots of every option in both engines were read; A and C look like Today, B's sparkles hold still. Nothing was checked on a real iPhone; the WebKit build here is a stand-in, likely software-rastered, not iOS Safari.
