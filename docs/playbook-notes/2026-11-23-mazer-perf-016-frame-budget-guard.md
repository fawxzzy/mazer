- WHAT changed: MAZER-PERF-016 phase 1 (frame-budget guard) and MAZER-PERF-006 (3x-phone canvas cap). (1) `tests/architecture/frame-budget-guard.test.ts` plus the recording-canvas helper `tests/architecture/support/recordingCanvas.ts`: a CI test (in the existing `tests/architecture` directory, so `verify.yml` already runs it) that pins what a smooth frame is made of: canvas operation counts for one trail rebuild on a long route at 3x (neon tube with and without a DOM, star dust), canvas operation counts for one star-field frame and the star list's storage reuse, the single-rect board wall fill, the trail rebuild cadence (rebuilds a second at 60/90/120/144 Hz for idle drift, glide, slow rebuild, static, and the colour clock at full and reduced motion), and the trail backing-store pixel budget on eight device profiles. (2) New pure module `src/render/canvasBackingBudget.ts` (`resolveCappedBackingResolution`: 1.6 M pixel budget, never below 2 device px per css px, never above the request), hooked into the one resolution line of `drawTrailCanvasSegments` in `src/scenes/MenuScene.ts` (HOT FILE: one import and 5 changed lines, nothing else).
- WHY it changed: Smoothness has been fixed symptom by symptom (#459, #488, #639, #660/#661, #667, #669) and nothing stops the next visual change from undoing them. Wall-clock time cannot be a CI gate (identical runs measured 3x apart on a busy machine), so the guard pins the operation counts and cadence that the time is made of. A 3x phone with a long route backs the trail with about 2.2 M pixels (918 x 2427 at level 300), and every rebuild clears, strokes, blurs and uploads all of them.
- Rule: A frame-budget failure means a change made a frame do more work. Either find the cheaper way, or raise the budget in the same PR with the new measured number and a sentence on why the picture needs it; never raise one just to turn a build green. Budgets carry about 25 percent margin over the value measured when the guard was written (each assertion's comment holds the measured number). The cadence budgets are literals on purpose (31 rebuilds a second for drift and glide, 23 and 31 for the colour clock) so changing the gate constants cannot silently move its own budget. Mutation-checked: adding a duplicate core pass fails the stroke budget, dropping the 33 ms floor to 8 ms fails seven cadence cases. What it does NOT measure: real frame time on a phone (use `scripts/analysis/profile-level-frame-cost.mjs` under throttle, and a phone), GPU compositing, bundle size (owned by `boot-payload-budget.test.ts`). Phase 1 covers draw, cadence and backing-store budgets only; a per-level full-scene draw-call budget needs a Phaser scene harness and is left to a later phase. The cap only changes the trail canvas: board tiles, player, markers, the main canvas, board size and hit-testing are untouched, and 1x and 2x devices and small canvases keep their exact current resolution.
- Evidence: Measured on the guard's fixture (1224 segments, 1207 chunks, 12 x 50 board at 3x): neon tube 1208 strokes on the target plus 1207 bloom strokes on a scratch canvas of 1/16 of the pixels with a 9 px blur, 0 blur writes on the target, 1 clipped bloom upscale; star dust 2415 strokes, 1027 sparkle fills, one keyline stroke; star field 240 drawImage, 1 resetTransform, 241 alpha writes, no path calls; idle drift and glide 30 rebuilds a second at 60 and 120 Hz. Backing cap: level 300 on a 393 x 852 phone goes from 2.23 M to 1.60 M pixels (3x to 2.54x); the whole viewport at 3x drops to about 2.18x instead of 3x. Interleaved in-page A/B at 3x (headless Chromium, proxy for a phone): the trail's cost scales with pixels, but the 1.6 M cap alone is worth only about 1.5 percent of a level-120 rebuild; most of the rebuild is stroke and blur work, which the operation budgets now pin. Crops of the trail at 3x, reference versus 1.6 M versus 1.0 M, all read as crisp; 1.0 M is slightly softer, so 1.6 M was kept. Nothing checked on a real phone.
